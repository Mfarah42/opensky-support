Weather worth following.

OpenSky paints your sky: your city, your horizon, the clouds the forecast is promising, lit the way the sun is lit right now. Underneath, a forecast you can follow. Pick a day that matters and OpenSky keeps every forecast it fetches for it, then tells you in one plain line what changed since you last looked. No account, no tracking, nothing leaves your phone.

FAQ

Frequently asked

The questions that come up most, answered plainly.

Where does the weather come from?

Current conditions, the hourly and daily forecast, minute-by-minute precipitation and official alerts come from Apple Weather (WeatherKit). Air quality and pollen come from Open-Meteo. Every card says which. OpenSky never invents a confidence number: when two estimates disagree it shows both, the spread, and when each was retrieved.

What does "Follow" do?

Tap a day in the seven-day list and tap Follow. From then on OpenSky keeps every forecast it fetches for that day. When the forecast crosses a threshold (rain appears or disappears, the timing shifts, the high or low moves) you get one notification that says exactly what moved, in numbers. There is a six-hour cooldown per followed day, quiet hours from 10 pm to 7 am defer to the morning, and several changes collapse into one notice. Choose how sensitive this is under Settings.

What is "What changed" comparing against?

The forecast you last looked at. Not the previous model run, not a fixed number of hours ago. If nothing crossed a threshold since your last open, the line stays away, and that quiet means something too.

What is Replay?

Open a day you follow and scrub through its saved forecasts. The chart shows what each forecast said and when, so you can watch a prediction drift toward the truth. Replay only compares forecasts for the same place, the same day and the same source, so a change of provider is never shown as a change of weather.

Why does my city have a different painting from my friend's?

Eighty cities have their own hand-painted landmark, chosen when you are within fifteen miles of it. Everywhere else gets a scene for the region: meadow, desert, prairie, pine forest, rocky coast, tropical coast or mountain valley. The paintings are illustrations, not a camera view. Clouds, the sun, the moon, rain and snow are drawn live from the forecast on top of them.

Does OpenSky need my location?

Only if you want the forecast for where you are. Location is requested once, used only while the app is open, and never leaves your phone. Deny it and OpenSky shows a default city you can change. You can also save up to ten cities and follow days in any of them.

What is special on iPhone Duo?

Open the phone and the painting stays on the left at full height, with the 48 hours running down it. The forecast fills the right and scrolls on its own. The controls sit in the side strip beside the clock. The cover screen shows the regular phone layout.

The widget says "Open to refresh". Why?

iOS decides when widgets may refresh. When the last update is more than an hour old the widget says so instead of showing stale numbers as current. Opening OpenSky refreshes it at once. Widgets follow the city selected on the Today tab.

Does OpenSky collect any data?

No. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no server of ours. Forecast requests go to Apple Weather and Open-Meteo for the coordinates you chose, and that is all they receive.
